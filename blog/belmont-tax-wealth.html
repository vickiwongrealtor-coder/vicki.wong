Proposition 13: The Long-Term Ownership Advantage

California's Proposition 13 limits annual property tax assessment increases to 2% — regardless of how much the property's market value increases. For long-term Belmont homeowners, this creates a compounding advantage that grows more valuable over time. A property purchased 20 years ago at a fraction of today's market value is assessed at that original value, not the current market value. This "lock-in" effect is one of the primary reasons Belmont's long-term homeowners are reluctant to sell, contributing to the chronic supply constraint that sustains prices.

Proposition 19: What Changed for Families

Proposition 19, effective since February 2021, fundamentally changed the rules for intergenerational property transfers. Previously, parents could transfer their property — including investment properties — to children while preserving the parent's low tax base. Under Prop 19, children who inherit a property but don't use it as their primary residence receive a reassessment to market value. For Belmont families with investment properties worth significantly more than their assessed value, this change has major estate planning implications.

Capital Gains: The Primary Residence Exclusion Strategy

The federal and California combined capital gains tax burden on the sale of a Bay Area primary residence can be substantial — effective rates of 30%+ on gains above the exclusion threshold are common for high-income California taxpayers. The $250,000/$500,000 primary residence exclusion (for single/married filers) requires 2 of 5 years of primary residence use. Planning the timing of a Belmont sale around this exclusion threshold is a decision that can be worth hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The 1031 Exchange in Belmont: Deferring Capital Gains on Investment Property

For Belmont investors holding appreciated investment properties, the 1031 exchange allows indefinite deferral of capital gains taxes by rolling proceeds into a replacement property. The rules are strict: 45 days to identify replacement property, 180 days to close. But properly executed, a series of 1031 exchanges can build substantial real estate wealth without triggering capital gains recognition until a final sale or estate distribution.

The "swap 'til you drop" strategy — repeatedly exchanging into larger properties — combined with the step-up in basis at death (which eliminates deferred capital gains for heirs) represents one of the most powerful wealth accumulation strategies available in California real estate.

Property Tax Appeals: When and How to Challenge Your Assessment

If your Belmont property was purchased at a time when the market was significantly lower than today, or if there are factors that would justify a below-market valuation (structural issues, location-specific negatives, access problems), filing a formal assessment appeal with Alameda County/Santa Clara County/San Mateo County (depending on location) may be worth exploring. Successful appeals can generate annual tax savings of $3,000–$15,000 that compound over years of ownership.
